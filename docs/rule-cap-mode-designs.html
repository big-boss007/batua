Edit Rule — Cap Mode Designs

Three approaches to surface the cap-vs-multiplier behavior in the Edit Rule modal. Each gives the merchant the choice; they differ in how prominent the decision is and how its consequences are visualized. Same example rule for all three: 5% on orders ≥ ₹800, max ₹450 per order.

A Inline dropdown · minimal  ·  B Radio cards · explicit  ·  C Tier-impact table · consequence-first
AInline dropdown — smallest UI change. Default stays hard cap; the merchant who wants something else opts in via a small dashed-underline link beneath the Max per order field.

On a ₹1,000 order a Bronze customer earns ₹50; a Gold (2×) earns ₹100; a Diamond (5×) would earn ₹250 — capped at ₹450 on every tier.
Pros: closest to current UI, almost invisible to most merchants who'll accept the default. Cons: the decision is buried under a small dropdown — merchants who care won't know it exists. Most won't even discover the alternative modes.
BRadio cards — forces a deliberate choice. Three cards directly below the cap field; each shows what a Diamond-tier ₹20,000 order would earn under that mode.

Hard cap selected: a Diamond customer on a ₹20,000 order earns ₹450, same as Bronze on a large order. The cap is the same across all tiers.
Pros: impossible to miss; each option's effect is shown numerically; no ambiguity. Cons: heavy real estate for a decision most merchants won't think about — risks making the modal feel intimidating. Best for a one-time decision at rule creation, less ideal on every edit.
CTier-impact table — shows the merchant exactly what each tier will earn given their current settings, with the cap-mode being a small toggle that updates the table live. Decision-by-consequence rather than decision-by-name.
